Module 1 of 4

Host Safety Responsibilities

✓ Hosts DO

  • Provide refrigerator space and refrigerate sandwiches immediately
  • Ensure sandwiches remain at or below 40°F
  • Receive and inspect sandwiches (sealed, labeled, cold to touch)
  • Coordinate sandwich transport
  • Contact your lead host if anything is unclear

✗ Hosts DO NOT

  • Open sealed sandwich bags
  • Accept sandwiches that feel warm or appear unsafe
  • Overfill refrigerators (airflow is essential!)
⚠️ Important

Never open sealed sandwich bags. This maintains hygiene and prevents cross-contamination.

Food Safety Foundations

Why Food Safety Matters

At The Sandwich Project, we serve many individuals who are more susceptible to foodborne illness:

  • Children and seniors
  • People with chronic health conditions or weakened immune systems
  • Many may not have health insurance or easy access to healthcare
⚠️ Critical

Food safety depends on strict consistency at every step. There are no shortcuts.

Three Pillars of Food Safety

🌡️
Temperature
Keep it cold — maintain the cold chain
🧼
Hygiene
Keep it clean — surfaces, hands, equipment
✨
Quality
Keep it neat — proper assembly matters
Module 2 of 4

The Cold Chain

The "cold chain" means keeping perishable food consistently cold from the moment it's purchased until it's delivered to recipients.

💡 Key Point

At every stage, minimize or eliminate breaks in the cold chain. Keep time outside refrigeration as short as possible.

Absolute Temperature Limits

40°F
Maximum Safe Temp
Above this, bacteria begin to grow
80°F
Danger Zone
Bacterial growth accelerates rapidly — food is unsafe
Module 2 of 4

The Two-Hour Rule ⏱️

⚠️ Critical Rule

Deli meat and cheese may only be above 40°F for a total of two hours, cumulatively.

This Is NOT "Two Hours at a Time"

It's two hours TOTAL, added up across the entire process. This includes all time the food is not refrigerated:

  • At the grocery store
  • During sandwich assembly
  • During transfer to and from coolers
  • Any time from the true source until volunteers picked it off the shelf

Why This Matters

  • As an organization, TSP is subject to food safety laws
  • Our recipients are more vulnerable than the average population
  • Recooling does not kill bacteria that have already grown
💡 Key Point

Two hours may sound like plenty, but it adds up fast. That's why we're strict at every step.

Module 2 of 4

Refrigeration Standards

1
Temperature Range — Refrigerators must maintain 34–38°F consistently
2
Air Circulation — Food items must not be packed tightly. Adequate airflow is essential.
3
Never Overfill — Overfilled refrigerators create warm spots, compromising safety
4
Door Discipline — Keep doors closed as much as possible
5
Dedicated & Clean — The TSP refrigerator should be used only for TSP sandwiches

Pro Tip: Freezers are great for getting sandwiches down to safe temperatures quickly before going into coolers for transport.

Module 2 of 4

Coolers & Transport

⚠️ Important Distinction

Coolers are NOT refrigerators. They maintain cold food — they do not cool warm food.

Coolers work through insulation, creating a barrier that slows heat transfer. They lack the circulation or active cooling to actually lower temperature.

✓ Coolers Work For

  • Getting ingredients home from the store
  • Transporting pre-chilled sandwiches with ice packs
  • Maintaining safe temps for 2-4 hours during active transport

✗ Coolers Do NOT Work For

  • Bringing warm food temperature down
  • Storing cold food for long periods
  • Making warm food safe again after the 2-hour rule is broken

Maximizing Cooler Performance

  • Ice packs everywhere — Place between layers and on top of sandwiches
  • Pack full — Full coolers with minimal air space stay colder longer
  • Keep lid closed — Every opening lets warm air in

Module 3 of 4

Handling Quality Issues

When volunteers arrive, watch for these issues:

🚫 Open or Torn Bags

  • Do NOT repackage
  • Politely refuse delivery, explaining TSP requires sealed packaging for safety
  • If uncomfortable refusing: quarantine (mark "DO NOT USE - OPEN BAG") and contact your lead host immediately

🏷️ Missing or Incorrect Labels

  • Ask volunteer to complete the TSP label on the spot
  • Verify label shows Tuesday evening or Wednesday
  • If they can't provide info, contact your lead host
⚠️ Warm, Moldy, or Spoiled Sandwiches

These cannot be accepted. They represent significant food safety risks. Sandwiches should feel cool to the touch on arrival.

Module 3 of 4

If You Cannot Accept Sandwiches

You have options when something isn't right:

Option A: Direct Refusal (Preferred)

If comfortable, kindly refuse delivery directly.

Suggested script: "I'm sorry, but for safety reasons, I can't accept these sandwiches. The Sandwich Project has strict quality and safety standards to protect our recipients."

Option B: The "Quarantine"

If uncomfortable refusing directly:

  1. Accept but isolate immediately
  2. Mark bags "DO NOT USE" with reason
  3. Contact lead host for disposal instructions
⚠️ Critical Reminders

Never place warm sandwiches into a cooler! Coolers maintain temperature — they don't cool things down.

No coolers in the trunk! Keep in the vehicle cabin where A/C circulates. Trunks can reach extreme temperatures.

Module 3 of 4

Thursday Protocol: Delivery & Transport

Thursday is delivery day. Proper packing ensures sandwiches remain safe.

1
Verify cold sandwiches — Only place sandwiches refrigerated/frozen for at least 15 minutes into a cooler
2
Layer with ice packs — Place between packages and on top. All packages should contact an ice pack.
3
Pack cooler tightly — Full coolers resist warming better than partially filled ones
4
Keep in vehicle cabin — Never the trunk. A/C helps maintain temperature.
5
Deliver promptly — Go straight to destination without delay
6
Refrigerate immediately — Transfer to refrigeration as soon as you arrive
Module 3 of 4

Critical Transport Rules

⚠️ Non-Negotiable Rules

These rules exist because breaking them makes food unsafe. No exceptions.

1
Never Pack a Hot Cooler — Coolers stored in hot conditions must cool completely before packing. A hot cooler will immediately warm sandwiches.
2
Never Put Warm Sandwiches in a Cooler — Sandwiches must be refrigerator-cold (below 40°F) before going in. Non-negotiable.
3
TSP Use Only — Don't use TSP coolers for raw meat, fish, or other non-TSP items to prevent cross-contamination.
4
No Packed Coolers Left in Vehicles — Coolers are for active transport only. Never leave them sitting in a parked car.
